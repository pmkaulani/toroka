# Research plan: does a "couldn't confirm, here is the evidence" checker change what people do?

**Owner:** Kaulani · **Status:** Draft · **Prepared:** 20 Oct 2026 · **Length:** 2 weeks

## 1. Objectives
Learn, with real behaviour rather than opinion:
1. How people currently decide whether a suspicious message is real, and what they do next (current process).
2. Whether they would stop and use a separate tool, and what would make them do it at the moment of the message.
3. How they react to an honest "couldn't confirm" result compared with a yes or no. (Highest design risk.)
4. Which message types are most common in their lives, to choose the first official sources to cover.
5. Whether pasting, sharing or forwarding feels most natural.

## 2. Assumptions under test (from the blueprint review)
| # | Assumption | Evidence so far | What would change my mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | People will paste or upload before paying | None | Most say they would not stop, or ask someone instead |
| A2 | Users value "couldn't confirm" over a yes or no | None | Most read it as "the tool failed" |
| A3 | Job, bursary and grant messages are the most urgent | Hypothesis | Shopping or M-PESA messages dominate |
| A4 | English plus Swahili is enough at launch | Plausible | Many real messages are mostly Sheng |
| A5 | The 333 forward habit is the model for intake | Indirect | People rarely forward |

## 3. Method
- **10 to 15 interviews**, 50 minutes each, in person or by call (guide: `interview-guide.md`).
- Inside each: a **six-message behavioural test** and a **paper reaction test** of three wordings for the unknown state.
- Optional second phase: a click-through prototype with 5 to 8 participants once a result card exists.

## 4. Participants (quotas, not a random sample)
| Group | Min | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lost money to a scam, or nearly did | 4 | Real stakes and recall |
| Job, bursary or grant seekers | 4 | Likely first wedge |
| Small sellers or online shoppers | 3 | Payment and shop flows |
| Older adults or feature-phone users | 2 | Reach gap |

Adults only (18+). Screener: received a suspicious message in the last 3 months; uses mobile money; comfortable being recorded or having notes taken. Mix of counties and devices. Recruit through your own network first, then ask each participant for one referral; note that this biases toward people like you, and say so in the report.

## 5. Ethics and data handling
- Plain-language consent in English and Swahili, read aloud. Participation is voluntary; they can skip any question.
- Never ask for PINs, passwords or full ID numbers. If a participant shows a real message, they cover or skip personal details.
- Record audio only with explicit consent. Store notes under a participant code; keep the code-to-person sheet separate.
- Ask separately whether they will donate an anonymised message to the evaluation set. Declining changes nothing.
- Delete recordings after synthesis. Get Kenyan data-protection advice before storing anything beyond notes.
- Thank-you in the form of airtime or similar; decide the amount from your budget.

## 7. Analysis
- Code each session against A1 to A5 and these tags: trigger, channel, who they ask, time to decide, evidence they trust, reaction to uncertainty, language.
- Affinity-map observations into themes; rank themes on an impact/effort matrix.
- Map the current journey (trigger, doubt, ask, search, decide, act, regret) and mark waste (see `../process/process-optimization.md`).
- Jobs to be done: write "When I get ___, I want to ___ so I can ___" statements only from observed behaviour.

## 8. Decision rules (suggested thresholds; adjust before you start)
- If fewer than half of participants say the result would change what they do, rework the result card before building more.
- If most participants read "couldn't confirm" as failure, test the other two wordings and the evidence-first layout.
- If one message category covers about half of what participants bring, make it the first registry wedge.
- If few people would open a separate tool, prioritise forward-to-check over the web app.

## 9. Risks
- Social-desirability bias ("yes I'd use it"): mitigated by the behavioural test and by asking what they did last time.
- Small, similar sample: report it as directional, not statistical.
- Recall errors: use the participant's own phone history where they are comfortable.

## 10. Deliverables
Research report (themes, insights, recommendations), a highlight reel of short quotes with consent, an updated assumptions table, and 50 consented, anonymised example messages for the evaluation set.
